A visual introduction to quantum optimization

How does a problem become a quantum algorithm?

Follow one small graph as it transforms into mathematics, spins, a quantum circuit and a probability distribution. No controls to operate. Just scroll, observe and connect the ideas.

↓   SCROLL TO FOLLOW THE TRANSFORMATION
One problem. Six connected ideas.We will use Max-Cut: divide a graph's vertices into two groups so that the total weight of edges crossing between them is as large as possible. QAOA is one way to search for a good partition.
01 / THE PROBLEM

It starts with a graph, not a quantum circuit.

Imagine three connected places. Split them into two groups. Every connection crossing between groups earns one point. The task is to maximize the total score.

C(x) = Σ wᵢⱼ · [xᵢ ≠ xⱼ]

Each vertex gets a binary label: 0 or 1.

02 / BINARY MODEL

Translate the goal into algebra.

A pair contributes when its labels differ. For binary variables this is xᵢ + xⱼ − 2xᵢxⱼ. Summing over edges gives the cut value.

C(x) = Σ wᵢⱼ(xᵢ + xⱼ − 2xᵢxⱼ)
minimize −C(x)

QUBO means Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization. We minimize the negative score because the standard form is a minimization.

03 / ISING MAPPING

Bits become spins.

Replace each binary variable with zᵢ = 1 − 2xᵢ. The two values are +1 and −1. In quantum notation, these become eigenvalues of Pauli-Z operators.

xᵢ = (1 − zᵢ)/2
HC = −3w/2 + (w/2)Σ ZᵢZⱼ

The cost Hamiltonian assigns an energy to every candidate partition.

04 / QUANTUM CIRCUIT

The Hamiltonian guides the circuit.

QAOA begins with all three qubits in an equal superposition. The cost unitary adds phases according to the graph objective. The mixer rotates qubits, allowing amplitudes to move between candidate bitstrings.

|ψ(γ, β)⟩ = UM(β) UC(γ) |+⟩⊗3

One cost layer plus one mixer layer is a QAOA layer. Repeating them gives depth p.

05 / STATE EVOLUTION

Interference reshapes the possibilities.

The circuit changes amplitudes of a superposition. Angles γ and β control these transformations; a classical optimizer can search for angles that improve the expected objective.

P(x) = |⟨x|ψ(γ, β)⟩|²

The bars represent measurement probabilities, not the cut score itself.

06 / MEASUREMENT

Measure, evaluate, improve.

Measurements produce bitstrings such as 001 or 110. Evaluate each sample using the original graph objective. A classical optimizer uses the expected cut value to update γ and β, then the cycle repeats.

E[C] = Σₓ P(x) C(x)

QAOA is a hybrid quantum–classical optimization loop.

THE BIG PICTURE

QAOA does not remove the modeling work.

The graph is first expressed as a classical objective, then mapped to an Ising cost Hamiltonian. That Hamiltonian defines a parameterized quantum circuit. Measurements return candidate bitstrings, and a classical optimizer updates the circuit parameters. Result quality depends on the model, circuit depth, optimizer and available hardware.
